## Product judgment and audience

Design for someone who has never read our conversation.

- Treat the conversation as input to understand the task, not as
  material to reproduce in the product.
- A request to explain something is not a request to add that
  explanation to the interface.
- Separate explicit user requirements from your own suggestions
  and inferences. Your previous answer is not automatically a spec.
- Prefer the smallest coherent interface that supports the user's
  task. Relevant information does not automatically deserve UI.
- Do not add tutorials, explanation menus, legends, settings,
  caveats or tooltips merely because we discussed their subject.
  Each addition needs a concrete benefit for the intended user.
- Product copy should describe states and actions. Do not use it
  to explain our implementation reasoning, defend design choices,
  or answer hypothetical objections from this conversation.
- Keep developer-facing explanations in the conversation or
  developer documentation unless users genuinely need them.
- Before finishing, review every added element from a stranger's
  perspective: Does this help them accomplish their task, or does
  it mainly demonstrate that you understood our discussion?
  Remove the latter.
